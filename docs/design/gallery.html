Three modern directions for the VRAM Sherpa overhaul

These replace the first three. You asked for current 2024+ looks (bento grids, glass, spatial depth) with no physical-instrument metaphors, no skeuomorphic detail and no flat colour blocks. Each board shows the results page for 24 GB at a 4k context with real models and figures: search, the answer, Top Picks, three model cards with the fit gauge and quantisation switcher, and the footer.

The three differ in what carries the design: D is a grid of tiles, E is layers of glass, F is depth above a floor. Each has its own typeface, logo mark and favicon, shadow and motion spec.

Pick one at Gate 2, or name a mix. Round 2 then covers every screen and state in the chosen direction.

D. Bento

A bento grid. Every block of the page is a tile with its own size and job, and the answer tile is the biggest.

Every block of the page is a tile with its own size and job. The answer is the biggest tile, the form and the three Top Picks sit beside and under it, and the cards are the same tile at two sizes (the third spans the full row). Verdict counts nest inside the answer tile as three small tiles. It answers "too dense" by giving each fact its own cell, and "generic" by being built on one grid idea throughout. Type is a variable grotesque whose optical size changes with the text size, so the 46 px headline is tight and the 14 px captions are sturdy.

One container, the tile, at three heights: recessed fields, tiles, raised on hover. No blur.

Watch out for
Because every block is a rounded tile, hierarchy depends on tile size and on the answer tile's verdict glow. The full-width third card uses a container query; where that is unsupported it falls back to the one-column card. The verdict colours on the count tiles are pale fills held up by icon shapes and labels.

E. Glass

Layered glass. Frosted panes float over a soft colour field, and the cards are lighter sheets resting on a pane.

Frosted panes float over a soft colour field. Inside each pane the cards are lighter sheets, so there are three readable layers: field, pane, sheet. The answer is the large light headline at the top of the right pane. Glass is the only direction where contrast depends on what sits behind the text, so every text colour was checked against the worst colour in the backdrop, then against a render with the backdrop forced to that colour.

Type
Outfit, variable weight, 32 KB
Layers
Backdrop field, blurred panes, lighter inset sheets. Blur 24 px desktop, 16 px phone.
Top Picks
A sticky rail inside the list pane on desktop; one collapsed row after the first card on a phone (Q-1, side rail)

Watch out for
Contrast on glass drove one change: the dark pane was 70% opaque and a render on the brightest backdrop colour measured 3.64:1, so it is now 82% and measures 5.52:1. Phones get 3 blurred layers instead of 5 (the answer pane and footer drop their blur). Solid fallbacks apply without backdrop-filter and under reduced transparency.

F. Spatial

Spatial depth. Windows float at different heights above a floor, lift toward you when touched, and tilt a few degrees with the pointer.

Windows float at different heights above a floor. The answer window is the highest and the Top Picks overlap its lower edge; the form is one plane down; cards sit on the plane and cast a faint shadow in their verdict colour. Touching a card lifts it and tilts it a few degrees toward the pointer, and the cards around it ease back. The depth comes from shadows, not blur, so only the header is glass.

Type
Instrument Sans, variable width and weight, 57 KB
Layers
Floor, plane, float, ornament (the glass header and small raised chips), lift. 1 blurred layer.
Top Picks
A raised tier that overlaps the answer window on desktop (Q-1, above the cards); one collapsed row after the first card on a phone (Q-1, after the first cards)

668 px on desktop (verdict and margin end at 735 px, target 800). 1048 px on a phone (verdict and margin end at 1275 px, target 1688).
Watch out for
Tilt and glare only run for a fine pointer and only when motion is allowed. Large layered shadows cost paint time on a long list, so the build should cap card shadow blur at 56 px and the 71-model list should not animate on load. The verdict-coloured cast shadow uses :has(); the build should put the verdict class on the card on the server.
